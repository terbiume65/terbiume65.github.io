MSc thesis at Mahidol

Predicting metastatic organotropism

My MSc thematic paper, published as code: pan-cancer models that ask whether a tumour has metastasised, where it spreads, and — reading the problem backwards — which primary site a metastasis came from. Six model families across three tasks. Binary prediction reached AUROC 0.71 and reverse prediction 50% accuracy; forward site prediction stayed close to the baseline.

About

I studied Global Health & Development at HKU, with a second major in Linguistics — which is what led, fairly directly, to the bankruptcy-prediction project above. A few of the essays behind that degree are on the writing samples page.

After HKU I spent the second half of 2022 as a research fellow at the UNDP's Human Development Report Office in New York, exploring datasets for the next Human Development Report and building the matching tools that ended up in the projects on this page.

Since then I've added an MBA at Anglia Ruskin (distinction) and an MSc in Biomedical and Health Informatics at Mahidol, and I'm now at the dissertation stage of a DBA at Edgewood University.

I'm studying biomedical sciences at CityU because I wanted the wet-lab and clinical grounding I didn't have — to understand disease from the bench and the bedside, and hopefully to contribute to a breakthrough for my own.

Currently fighting cancer — advanced-stage desmoplastic small round cell tumour — and doing postgraduate work while receiving treatment. If remission is achieved, I'm looking for remote roles or casual work with international organizations.

Away from work I read a lot, like languages and linguistics, have a lasting fondness for Tigger, and play a fair amount of mahjong — enough to have written the basics up as a page for people learning the game.
